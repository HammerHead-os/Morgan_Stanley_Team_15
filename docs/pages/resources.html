For families

Useful resources

Real Hong Kong subsidies, services, and support networks for families raising a child with Down syndrome, autism, or another neurodivergence. Tap a question to open it.

This is a starting point, not legal or medical advice — amounts and rules change, so confirm details on the linked official page before you apply.

Getting started Your child was just diagnosed with autism or Down syndrome — what happens next? +

Start with your Maternal and Child Health Centre (under 5) or the Student Health Service (school age) — or your paediatrician — for an initial referral. They refer you to the Department of Health's Child Assessment Service, where a developmental paediatrician, clinical psychologist, or child psychiatrist makes the formal diagnosis.

Once diagnosed, ask the assessing professional or a medical social worker to refer your child into the Social Welfare Department's Central Referral System for Rehabilitation Services — this is what places your child in line for subsidised early-intervention support (see the next card). The wait from first concern to receiving intensive service can run 13–20 months, so refer as early as you can.

Source: Department of Health — Autism Spectrum Disorder

Early intervention Get on the pre-school rehabilitation waiting list — and a training subsidy while you wait +

Once your child is in the Central Referral System, they're eligible for a subsidised place at an Early Education and Training Centre, a Special Child Care Centre, or On-site Pre-school Rehabilitation Services (OPRS) — therapy delivered right inside kindergartens and child care centres, with about 8,000 places across Hong Kong.

Waiting lists are long. While you wait, families that meet the income test can apply for a training subsidy so your child can get therapy from a private "Recognised Service Provider" in the meantime, instead of going without support.

Source: Social Welfare Department — On-site Pre-school Rehabilitation Services

Money Disability Allowance — a monthly cash allowance for your child +

If your child is certified as severely disabled by the Department of Health or the Hospital Authority — most children with Down syndrome or a more significant autism diagnosis qualify — a medical social worker can apply for Disability Allowance on their behalf. It is not means-tested.

As of February 2026: HKD 2,140 a month (Normal Disability Allowance) or HKD 4,280 a month (Higher rate, for those who need constant attendance from someone else), plus a HKD 345 a month Transport Supplement for ages 12–64.

Source: Social Welfare Department — Social Security Allowance Scheme

Transport Half-fare MTR, buses, and minibuses +

Ask your social worker to arrange a Personalised Octopus with "Persons with Disabilities Status" alongside your Disability Allowance application — it gives your child half-fare travel on the MTR, franchised buses, and green minibuses, any time, any day.

Source: MTR — Fare Concession Scheme for Eligible Persons with Disabilities

Days out Free Ocean Park entry for your child, half price for one adult +

Bring your child's Registration Card for People with Disabilities — or the Notification of Successful Application for Disability Allowance — plus their HKID to the ticket counter. Your child gets in free, and one accompanying adult gets half-price admission.

Source: Ocean Park — Admission Ticket & Special Offers

Days out Skip the physical queue at Hong Kong Disneyland +

Disneyland's Disability Access Service (DAS) lets guests with disabilities — including autism — get a return time for rides instead of standing in the physical line, which can make a real difference for sensory or attention needs. Register at Guest Services when you arrive; there's no separate ticket to buy.

Source: Hong Kong Disneyland — Disability Access Service

Education Free textbooks and an internet subsidy for school +

If your household income qualifies, the Student Finance Office's School Textbook Assistance Scheme and Subsidy Scheme for Internet Access Charges cover textbooks and home internet for primary and secondary students. One combined "Household Application Form for Student Financial Assistance Schemes" covers both — apply each school year.

Source: Working Family and Student Financial Assistance Agency — Financial Assistance Schemes

Down syndrome community Hong Kong Down Syndrome Association — parent networks and newborn support +

HKDSA runs the Bradbury Parents Resource Centre — care-skills workshops and stress-relief support for caregivers — and the SHARE Project, with five regional parent networks across Hong Kong where families meet regularly for mutual support. They also work directly with Hong Kong and Kowloon hospitals to reach parents of newborns diagnosed with Down syndrome.

Source: Hong Kong Down Syndrome Association

Autism community JC A-Connect and Heep Hong Society — school-based autism support +

The Jockey Club Autism Support Network (JC A-Connect) is a Hong Kong-wide programme — run with Heep Hong Society, the University of Hong Kong, and the Education Bureau — built for students with ASD in mainstream schools and their families, with training and school-based support. Heep Hong Society also runs Autism Awareness Week every April.

Source: JC A-Connect — Jockey Club Autism Support Network
